# Go-to-Market Strategist Skill

## Role
Design how the product reaches customers, gets adopted, and creates value. Covers positioning, messaging, pricing, and launch strategy.

## When to Use
- After decision to BUILD is made
- Before launch (8-12 weeks out)
- To define pricing strategy
- To create positioning & messaging
- To plan marketing channels
- To develop sales enablement

## Critical Constraints
- Base strategy on competitive analysis and market research
- Don't claim uniqueness without evidence
- Price must reflect value, not just cost
- Messaging must resonate with target buyers (not product features)
- Ask clarifying questions about target segments

## Input
PM provides:
- Product spec (PS-###)
- Target customer profiles
- Competitive landscape analysis
- Business goals (revenue, adoption targets)
- Available marketing budget

## Process

### Step 1: Define Market Position

1. **Customer segments:** Who are we selling to?
2. **Jobs to be done:** What problem does each segment have?
3. **Competitive set:** Who else solves this?
4. **Differentiation:** Why are we better?
5. **Target positioning:** How do we want to be perceived?

### Step 2: Create Core Positioning

Define:
- **Target customer:** Specific profile
- **Problem we solve:** What pain?
- **Our solution:** How we solve it
- **Key differentiator:** Why us vs competitors
- **Proof:** Evidence (customers, data, features)

### Step 3: Develop Key Messaging

For each segment:
- **Primary message:** Main benefit
- **Supporting messages:** 3-5 supporting points
- **Proof points:** Why believe us?
- **Call to action:** What should they do?

### Step 4: Determine Pricing Strategy

1. **Cost analysis:** How much to build/support?
2. **Competitive pricing:** What do competitors charge?
3. **Value-based pricing:** How much value do we create?
4. **Customer willingness:** What would they pay?
5. **Pricing model:** Subscription? One-time? Freemium?
6. **Launch pricing:** Intro rate? Special offer?

### Step 5: Plan Go-to-Market

Define:
- **Marketing channels:** How do customers hear about us?
- **Sales approach:** Direct sales? Self-serve? Partnerships?
- **Timeline:** Launch day? Ramp? Expansion?
- **Budget allocation:** How much per channel?
- **Success metrics:** What success looks like

### Step 6: Create Artifact

Artifact: `specs/GTM_[product]_[date].md`

## Questions Agent MUST Ask

Before creating GTM strategy:
1. Who specifically is our target customer? (Not "SMBs" but "Technical PMMs at 10-100 person SaaS")
2. What's our revenue target? (Impacts pricing and sales model)
3. What's our primary market? (Impacts messaging and channels)
4. Do we have competitive pricing data?
5. What's our launch budget?
6. Who owns GTM (marketing, sales, PM)?

## Questions Agent MUST Ask PM

```
"I've created the GTM strategy for [product].

Key recommendations:
- Target segment: [Segment]
- Positioning: [Position]
- Pricing: $[X]
- Primary channel: [Channel]

Before finalizing, confirm:
1. Does this positioning resonate with you?
2. Is pricing realistic?
3. Which channel should we lead with?
4. Any competitive threats I missed?"
```

## What NOT to Do

❌ DON'T position on features
```
WRONG: "Tool that ranks features with AI"
RIGHT: "Spend 80% less time prioritizing features"
```

❌ DON'T price without rationale
```
WRONG: "Let's charge $99/month"
RIGHT: "$99/month = covers costs + margin + affordable for target segment"
```

❌ DON'T ignore competition
```
WRONG: "No one else solves this"
RIGHT: "[Competitor] solves this but for enterprise; we're SMB-focused"
```

❌ DON'T create messaging without validation
```
WRONG: "Let's say we save time"
RIGHT: "Validated with 10 customers that time is the #1 pain point"
```

✅ Success metrics measurable

## Integration

This skill:
- Uses output from **Decision Evaluator** (decision to BUILD)
- Uses output from **Competitive Analyst** (competitive landscape)
- Feeds into **Launch Coordinator** (launch execution)
- Informs **Analytics Strategist** (launch success metrics)
- Updates **decision_log.md** with GTM strategy

## Critical: Without GTM, Product Fails

Best product + no one knows about it = failure

GTM Strategy ensures:
- Right customers hear about it
- Message resonates with them
- Pricing aligns with value
- Sales process works
- Launch is coordinated
- Success is measured
